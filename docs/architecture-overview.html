Observed framing Working hypothesis

Why run this experiment?

Starting question: how could Galadreal introduce AI-driven decision support without repeating previous hallucination failures?

Three initial concerns

1
Wrong evidence

The agent could select datapoints, geographies, or analyses that do not match the real decision.

2
Unsupported calculation or explanation

The model could invent arithmetic, rankings, or narrative claims beyond what evidence supports.

3
Irreproducible recommendation

The product could present an answer that no engineer or customer can reconstruct from sources.

Intent of the build I built a narrow retail-location prototype to test architectural boundaries, not to design the final Galadreal product.

Illustrative scenario Scenario only

A national apparel retailer wants to compare candidate regions for a new store — for example Burlington, South Burlington, and Winooski — prioritizing growth and accessibility over current market size.

Note. Retail was chosen because Calandra mentioned location intelligence as a potential use case. The retailer and its economics are illustrative. This exercise uses no proprietary retailer data and is not affiliated with any retailer.

Hypothesis 1 — Separate deciding what to investigate from deciding what is true

An agent should manage the decision process — understanding the question, identifying missing information, proposing approved analyses, and explaining results — while Atlas and deterministic analytical services remain responsible for facts, calculations, and comparability.

A model that both chooses the evidence and determines what is true is difficult to govern. The prototype tests whether those powers can be separated. Not yet validated by StateBook

Agentic responsibilities

Hypothesis
  • Interpret the business objective
  • Ask material clarification questions
  • Propose metrics and analytical capabilities
  • Identify unsupported data requirements
  • Propose revisions to an analysis plan
  • Translate validated evidence for an executive audience

Deterministic & evidence responsibilities

Hypothesis
  • Resolve approved geographies
  • Retrieve Atlas evidence
  • Validate source, period, unit, and geography
  • Perform arithmetic
  • Normalize and rank
  • Assess sufficiency
  • Preserve provenance
  • Produce reproducible outputs

What the exercise demonstrated

Only mechanisms that are implemented in the repository are listed here. Demonstrated in prototype — not yet validated against StateBook production architecture

1. Governed planning Demonstrated

  • Natural-language objective becomes a typed plan proposal
  • Material assumptions are exposed on the plan
  • User approval is required before Atlas execution
  • Revisions create a new plan version rather than mutating the approved one

2. Evidence-bound Atlas workflow Demonstrated

  • Metrics come from a verified registry gated on live-checked datapoints
  • Atlas calls preserve source, period, geography, and raw-request provenance
  • Missing or incompatible evidence stays visible (not silently dropped)
  • The agent does not create factual values — only EvidenceItems from Atlas responses

3. Deterministic analytics Demonstrated

  • Normalization, weighting, scoring, sensitivity, and reproducibility hashes run outside the model
  • Sufficiency logic can withhold a ranking when evidence does not distinguish candidates reliably
  • LLM is optional for correctness; deterministic planner is the reference path

4. Evidence-bound explanation Demonstrated

  • Narrator and assistant receive a validated fact sheet / context pack
  • Unsupported company financial forecasts are refused before a model call when detected
  • Generated figures not present in evidence are rejected and replaced deterministically
Known limitation Pattern-based injection and forecast detectors are prototype controls, not a complete security boundary. Sessions are in-memory and unauthenticated — appropriate for a local demo, not multi-replica production.
Engineering evidence (secondary): fixed workflow stages in orchestration/workflow.py; offline Python suite and frontend contract tests; demo-token geography limited to Burlington–South Burlington metro. Counts of layers or tests are not the value proposition.

What could realistically be built first?

Three candidate vertical slices — not a recommendation.

Option A — Evidence-backed result explainer Lower risk

Input

An existing Atlas or Calypso result.

AI role

Explain the result, identify limitations, answer grounded follow-up questions.

Why lower risk

No autonomous data selection or calculation.

Questions

  • Is there a customer need?
  • Can Calypso expose a structured evidence context?

Option B — Guided Atlas query builder Medium risk

Input

A natural-language business question.

AI role

Propose approved datapoints, geographies, and filters.

Deterministic role

Validate and execute the query.

Why medium risk

The model affects evidence selection, but only through a registry and approval.

Questions

  • Is datapoint discovery a real user pain?
  • Is there already an internal semantic / query builder?

Option C — Governed decision workflow Higher scope

Input

A strategic objective such as retail site selection.

AI role

Clarify the decision, construct a plan, orchestrate approved analyses, explain.

Why higher scope

Needs domain metrics, analytical services, product design, and often customer-specific data.

Questions

  • Is there a specific customer workflow ready?
  • What deterministic services already exist?
